What you do out there

Asteroids are terrain rather than targets: shoot one long enough and it comes apart into ore and upgrades, while the gun overheats and the hull doesn't grow back. A full hold is worth nothing until it's docked at the mothership, and the flight home is the part of the run you can lose.

Made of nothing

The belt is grown from a seed and every note of the soundtrack is played by a sequencer, so there isn't one asset in the build: no sprites, no models, no audio files. It's vanilla JavaScript and a canvas, with no runtime libraries and two renderers behind one interface: wireframe or dithered pixels, swappable mid-flight.

Questions

What do I need to play it?#

A browser, on whatever the browser is running on. It's a canvas and under half a megabyte of JavaScript: no WebGL, no plugin, no install, no account, and nothing is fetched after the page itself, because the belt and the soundtrack are grown from a seed rather than loaded. Any current Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari will do.

Does it run on Linux, SteamOS or the Steam Deck?#

In the browser, yes, and today. beltnine.curzel.it is the demo, and nothing in it knows or cares which OS is under the browser. On a Deck that means desktop mode and any browser you have there.

The full game isn't out yet. When it ships to Steam it ships with a native x86_64 Linux binary (Ubuntu 22.04 or SteamOS 3 and later), so there's no Proton in the way and no compatibility tool to go and pick.

Steam Deck Verified is Valve's rating rather than mine, and it's awarded after they review the build. I'm not claiming it before it's earned, so treat the Deck as untested until the badge on the store page says otherwise.

Which Windows, and which Mac?#

Windows 10 64-bit or later. macOS 12 Monterey or later on Apple Silicon: there's no Intel build, and that's a decision rather than a thing still coming. Either one wants about 500 MB on disk, 4 GB of memory, and no graphics card in particular: integrated is fine. The game is the half megabyte above; the other 499 are the browser engine it's wrapped in.

Does it work on a phone or a tablet?#

It does. Touch controls (two sticks, a trigger and the roll keys) come up on first touch and are sized in millimetres rather than pixels, so they're the same size on every screen. Hold it in landscape; the game will ask you to.

Do I need a controller?#

Not to fly alone: keyboard and mouse is what it was built on, and pads are fully supported if you'd rather. Split-screen is where it matters: up to four pilots share one screen, and every pilot after the first needs a controller of their own.
