BELT NINE

MINE THE BELT. TAKE IT HOME.
A first-person game about mining asteroids. Shoot a rock until it comes apart, take the ore and whatever it was hiding, fill your hold, and fly it back to the mothership — which is the only place ore turns into anything. Up to four pilots on one screen.

Every screenshot here was taken with the game playing itself.

What you do out there

  • Take rocks apart. Asteroids are terrain, not targets: shoot one long enough and it shatters into smaller rocks, and a shatter pays out in ore and in upgrades — a bigger hold, more barrels, better radiators, an inertial coupler.
  • Watch the heat. The gun overheats, the hull doesn't grow back on its own, and there are hostiles in the belt who are also flying.
  • Fly it home. A full hold is worth nothing until it's docked, and the trip back is the part of the run you can lose.
  • Bring friends. Two, three or four pilots split-screen on one screen. Friendly fire is on by default. Every pilot after the first needs a controller.
  • Pick a belt. The world is grown from a seed — ?seed=belt names one, and the same seed is the same belt for everybody.
  • Play it however. Keyboard and mouse, a controller, or touch on a phone. English and Italian.

Three ways to draw it

There's a THEME row in the settings with three values, and they aren't filters — they're three renderers behind one interface, drawing the same scene with the same projection. A palette-indexed framebuffer with Bayer dithering, a wireframe stroked at your display's own resolution, and a grid of characters. You can swap them mid-flight.

I wrote up how that works, and why the ASCII one had to stop being a filter, over here: One scene, three renderers.
